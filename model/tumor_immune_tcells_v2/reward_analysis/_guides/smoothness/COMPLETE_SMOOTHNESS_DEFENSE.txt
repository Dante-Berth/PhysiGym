### Why Penalize Action Smoothness?

The smoothness penalty in the reward function encodes three coupled constraints:

**1. Biological timescale:** T cells require ~60-120 minutes to execute a 
coordinated attack (activation, proliferation, migration, killing). Our simulation 
uses 15-minute timesteps (dt_gym from PhysiCell config). Thus, action_repeat ≥ 4 
(60 minutes) is necessary to provide immune cells the required window. However, 
action_repeat = 6 (90 minutes) is optimal, suggesting that even longer dwell 
times benefit immune coordination—likely enabling more thorough cell-to-cell 
signaling and proliferation.

**2. Signal-to-noise ratio:** Rapid changes in drug concentration (AR < 5, every 
15-75 minutes) constitute noise to the immune system's chemical sensing mechanisms. 
T cells cannot distinguish a true environmental signal from oscillation. Our 
hyperparameter search (Figure 4.1) demonstrates this: tumor control collapses at 
AR < 5 (−9 cells killed) but recovers dramatically at AR ≥ 5 (−55 to −79 cells). 
The smoothness penalty forces the agent to respect this constraint.

**3. Credit assignment:** In reinforcement learning, temporal credit assignment 
becomes ambiguous when many actions occur before outcomes manifest. With short 
action_repeat, the agent makes 6-7 decisions per 90-minute window while tumor 
effects accumulate, scattering credit across multiple actions. The resulting 
gradient signal is noisy (std=14.1 at AR=1). With action_repeat = 6, the agent 
commits to a single action for the full effect window, enabling clear credit 
attribution and faster, more stable learning.

These three constraints—biological responsiveness, immune signal recognition, 
and gradient clarity—all align at action_repeat = 6. The smoothness penalty is 
not an arbitrary regularizer but a structural feature that enforces coherence 
between the agent's decision timescale and the biological system's dynamics.
